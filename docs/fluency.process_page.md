# Process Page
*(Standard Operating Procedures)*

## Process Overview

Fluency’s work gets done through a repeatable product and operations cycle that moves from customer need identification to design, build, validation, release, and support. The process is automated wherever possible, with quality, security, and operational checks built into every stage so the product can improve without sacrificing reliability.

## 1. Operating Rhythm

### Cadence & Rituals
- Planning: Weekly roadmap and priority review.
- Execution: Continuous delivery with short development cycles and task-based execution.
- Review: Demo reviews, retrospectives, incident reviews, and security reviews.

### Decision-Making
- Ownership: Product, engineering, and go-to-market decisions are owned by the relevant functional lead, with final alignment from company leadership.
- Criteria: Decisions balance speed, product quality, security, customer value, and operational simplicity.

## 2. Product & Design Process

### Discovery
- Inputs: Customer feedback, product usage signals, support issues, sales conversations, and partner input.
- Artifacts: Problem statements, requirements notes, feature outlines, user stories, and acceptance criteria.

### Design
- Activities: Wireframes, workflow mapping, prototype reviews, and edge-case analysis.
- Quality Gates: Design approval, brand consistency review, and compliance review when needed.

## 3. Build & Review Process

### Development
- Branching Model: Short-lived branches tied to a specific task or feature, with work merged back after review and validation.
- Practices: Code review, automated checks, test-first development where practical, and feature flag use for controlled rollout.

### Code Review
- Reviewers: At least one peer reviewer and a technical owner for the relevant area.
- Criteria: Correctness, readability, test coverage, security impact, performance, and maintainability.

### Testing
- Levels: Unit tests, integration tests, end-to-end tests, performance checks, and security-focused validation.
- Automation: Tests must pass automatically before merge or release approval.

## 4. Security, Quality & Release Process

### Pre-Merge Quality Gates
- Static Checks: Linting, static analysis, dependency checks, and required automated status checks.
- Manual Steps: Security review or stakeholder approval only when risk or scope requires it.

### Release
- Strategy: Phased release with controlled rollout and clear rollback paths.
- Environments: Development, staging, and production, with promotion only after passing defined checks.
- Rollback: Revert the last stable version quickly if errors, regressions, or security issues appear.

### Post-Release Monitoring
- Signals: Error rates, latency, usage trends, workflow success rates, alert quality, and security telemetry.
- Response: On-call or designated responder handles issues by severity with escalation when needed.

## 5. Customer & Feedback Loop

### Support & Incidents
- Channels: Email, ticketing, and partner escalations.
- SLAs: Response and resolution targets vary by severity, with urgent issues handled first.

### Learning & Iteration
- Feedback Intake: Support logs, product notes, customer calls, partner input, and internal issue tracking.
- Incorporation: Feedback is reviewed during planning and folded into roadmap priorities, bug fixes, workflow refinements, and release planning.

## 6. Why This Process Works (MOST CRITICAL SECTION)

### Core Strength
This operating model works because it combines fast iteration with strong automation and quality gates, allowing Fluency to improve the product quickly while keeping security, reliability, and customer trust intact.

### Evidence of Effectiveness
The process supports repeatable delivery, faster issue detection, fewer manual handoffs, safer releases, and a tighter feedback loop between customer needs and product changes. It also scales well because the same workflow can be applied consistently across features, teams, and partner-driven deployments.

## Process Summary

Fluency’s process is designed to move ideas into secure, usable product outcomes with minimal friction and high repeatability. By combining automation, structured review, and continuous feedback, the team can ship improvements quickly while maintaining quality and operational discipline.
