\# Produce Page

\## Style and Production Guidelines for Fluency



\### Purpose

This page defines the visual style, brand tone, and production rules for Fluency’s marketing and business-plan materials. The goal is to keep every page, deck, and asset consistent with Fluency’s positioning as an AI SIEM and autonomous SOC platform.



\### Brand Direction

Fluency should feel technical, modern, and operationally serious. The site emphasizes behavioral analytics, in-stream processing, autonomous workflows, context-rich alerts, and real-time detection, so the visual system should communicate speed, clarity, and confidence.



\### Tone

Use clear, concise, and credible language. The voice should sound security-focused, intelligent, and practical, with a strong emphasis on outcomes such as faster investigations, lower noise, and reduced operational cost.



\### Visual Style

\- Clean, minimal layouts with strong hierarchy.

\- Dark, enterprise-friendly design with high contrast.

\- Use structured sections, short blocks of copy, and simple iconography.

\- Highlight automation, detection, and workflow concepts with diagrams or process visuals.

\- Favor modern cybersecurity aesthetics over playful or decorative styling.



\### Color Scheme

Fluency’s public materials do not publish a formal brand palette on the reviewed pages, so the production system should use a secure, enterprise cyber palette inspired by the product positioning.  

Recommended palette:

\- Primary: Deep navy or near-black for trust and seriousness.

\- Secondary: Electric blue or cyan for technical energy and action.

\- Accent: Teal or green for success states, automation, and resolved workflow.

\- Neutral: Cool grays for body text, panels, and supporting UI elements.

\- Warning: Amber or orange for alerts, exceptions, and escalation states.



\### Typography

\- Headings should be bold, modern sans-serif, and highly legible.

\- Body text should be a clean sans-serif with generous spacing.

\- Use short headings and concise subheads to support scanability.

\- Avoid ornate fonts or overly stylized type treatments.



\### Layout Rules

\- Prefer one idea per section.

\- Use short paragraphs and bullet lists.

\- Keep dense technical content broken into manageable blocks.

\- Use tables for comparisons, product grouping, or workflow mapping.

\- Maintain strong whitespace so the page feels precise and easy to read.



\### Content Style

Fluency’s site stresses behavioral detection, UEBA clustering, gap detection, AI-driven analysis, and seamless integration.  

Production should reflect that by organizing content around:

\- Problem.

\- Behavioral insight.

\- Automation.

\- Analyst impact.

\- Business outcomes.



\### Messaging Priorities

\- Security decisions require understanding behavior.

\- Autonomy depends on strong detection.

\- Traditional SIEMs create noise; Fluency reduces it.

\- AI workflows improve investigation speed and accuracy.

\- The platform is built for SMB through enterprise scale.

\- Integration should feel quick and frictionless.



\### Supporting Assets

Recommended asset types for the business plan and pitch materials:

\- Title slides with strong product positioning.

\- Workflow diagrams showing alert-to-action progression.

\- Architecture illustrations for behavioral analytics and autonomous SOC operations.

\- One-page summaries for sales and partner use.

\- Simple charts showing cost reduction, MTTA improvement, and false-positive reduction.



\### Post-Production

After drafting, review all pages for:

\- Consistent terminology.

\- Consistent capitalization of product and company names.

\- Alignment with the behavioral analytics and autonomous SOC message.

\- Concise formatting and reduced repetition.

\- Visual balance between strategy, product, and execution sections.



\### Production Notes

Fluency is based in Rockville, Maryland, and the team describes itself as hands-on, collaborative, and results-oriented.  

That should be reflected in production by using direct language, practical visuals, and a disciplined structure that feels operational rather than promotional.  

The final output should support both investor-facing and partner-facing materials while reinforcing Fluency’s core promise of simpler, smarter security operations.
